# Future

This document contains ideas deliberately **outside the first Urumea MVP**. Items here are not commitments and should not be implemented merely because they are listed.

## Historical institutions and obligations

- churches, monasteries, parishes, and ecclesiastical careers
- tithes
- rents and dues
- customary rights
- lordship and patronage
- fueros and chartered towns
- royal authority and changing political jurisdictions
- service and military obligations

## Social power and conflict

- reputation with different households and institutions
- favours and reciprocal labour
- patron-client relationships
- powerful lineages / Ahaide Nagusiak
- feuds and banderizo conflict
- raids, war, insecurity, and protection
- legal disputes and arbitration
- inheritance disputes

Violence should remain a consequence affecting real people and households rather than becoming a disposable-unit warfare loop.

## Deeper economy

- local and regional markets
- prices driven by access, scarcity, and demand
- transport and pack animals
- specialized crafts
- milling
- iron mining
- charcoal production
- ferrerías / ironworks
- fishing
- shipbuilding
- port economies
- credit and debt
- hired labour
- apprenticeships
- long-distance trade

## Agricultural and ecological change

- more detailed crop calendars
- soil fertility and fallow
- pasture quality
- transhumance/seasonal livestock movement
- woodland regeneration and depletion
- erosion and land-use consequences
- new crops becoming historically available
- maize and later early-modern agricultural transformation
- demographic carrying-capacity changes

## Technology and knowledge diffusion

No conventional research tree.

Possible systems:

- techniques spread through people, markets, institutions, and neighboring regions;
- craftspeople carry knowledge when they migrate;
- adoption requires suitable resources, skills, capital, and incentives;
- technologies can reshape labour demand and settlement patterns;
- literacy and written administration gradually become relevant.

## Culture and language

- language use by household/place/network
- naming practices changing by time and context
- clothing and material culture
- architectural change
- religious practice
- customary law and practice
- multilingual trade and institutional networks

These should overlap rather than collapse into a single culture or assimilation meter.

- Jesuit and other early-modern networks where historically appropriate

## Landscape transformation

- roads changing travel cost
- bridges and fords

## Household memory and narrative

- named heirlooms and possessions
- historical ownership records for land/buildings
- family stories generated from simulation events
- notable ancestors
- remembered favors and grievances
- household chronicle
- campaign-end family history
- exportable genealogy/chronicle

## More nuanced household autonomy

- adult ambitions/preferences
- disagreements between household members
- relatives making unsolicited decisions
- semi-independent cadet households
- negotiations over marriage and migration
- autonomous neighboring households with their own economic goals

## Additional campaigns

Potential campaigns can explore different regions and transformations of the Basque world, for example:

- other Gipuzkoan valleys
- Bizkaia
- Álava
- Navarre
- Labourd
- Lower Navarre
- Soule
- late-medieval lineage conflicts
- iron-producing valleys
- Atlantic/coastal commercial growth
- 16th–17th century agricultural and demographic transformation

Campaigns should reuse the same simulation engine while changing geography, institutions, occupations, technology availability, demographic parameters, naming practices, and historical events.
